Real cases

Peace is not fantasy. It has been achieved through diplomacy in very difficult situations.

Article 33 of the UN Charter

The Charter requires parties to a dispute to seek a peaceful solution first: negotiation, enquiry, mediation, conciliation, arbitration, judicial settlement, or recourse to regional bodies. That is why the simulation above follows this order: de-escalate, build trust, then negotiate.

The Camp David Accords (1978)

Egyptian President Anwar Sadat and Israeli Prime Minister Menachem Begin met under the sponsorship of US President Jimmy Carter, leading to the Egypt-Israel peace treaty of 1979. Lesson: a mediator can be a state, and succeeds when both sides accept it.

The Good Friday Agreement (1998)

It ended decades of violence in Northern Ireland, after negotiations chaired by US Senator George Mitchell. Lesson: the agreement included all the main parties, not only the moderates.

The Algiers Agreement between Ethiopia and Eritrea (2000)

After a border war between the two countries (1998-2000), Algeria, together with the Organization of African Unity, sponsored talks that ended with the Algiers Agreement in December 2000. In 2018 the two countries formally declared the end of the state of war. Lesson: peace can take many years after the signing.
